## Page 6

1) Thesis: This is your elevator pitch and sets the tone for the rest of what you discuss

Your pitch is your way to synthesize all your research into a money generating idea, but if a tree falls in the forest with no one around, does it make a sound? That's why it's so crucial to be concise with your pitch and begin with your conclusion before you lose the audience.

**Thesis:**

Fundamental misunderstanding: Given the capital- intensive nature of the business, CAR needs to continuously seek funding for its fleet, causing fixed cost base to increase rapidly as new debt refinancing gets more expensive

Interest Expense is aggressively mismodelled - rising rates and a slowing economy should cause the stock to retrate as true earnings power is destroyed. Sellside is stuck on the I/S and not thinking about the balance sheet impacts of their I/S projections Top- line and margins weaken because of travel and used car slowdowns, pressuring ability to generate returns

**Short Thesis:**

The market is overestimating the PINS' ability to grow ARPU: ad market saturation, price efficiency driven by large players, and structurally worse metrics in key customer segments will lead to slower than expected ARPU and Monetizable MAU growth.

Re- rate to occur on multiple quarter earnings underperformance driven by ARPU that fails to converge towards peers.

The CAR thesis makes it very clear that we're playing for a miss due to mismodeled interest expense and a softening travel market. The rest of the pitch can get into the details, but we already know how the analyst's research led to variance vs. the street.

The PINS thesis highlights the key metric that is mismodeled (ARPU) and why. The analyst also notes that it won't just be numbers that fall (multiple as well).

## Page 8

2b) Idea/Price Target Summary

PM teams approach valuation in different ways

**Two Primary Valuation Methods:**

**1. Relative Valuation - Multiples**
- Determine the metric(s) that matter (e.g. P/E, EV / EBITDA, EV / FCF)- Look at multiple relative to the company's comps and to the company's historical multiple- Good for shorter term gauge of value

**2. Intrinsic Valuation - DCF**
- Very sensitive to inputs and terminal values- The value of any financial asset is the present value of the expected cash flows- Better as a longer-term gauge of value- Some teams use as a primary valuation method, others as a sanity check

4) Catalysts/Event Path

It's great to have a view on a business and what happens to numbers, but just as important is figuring out how you will get paid. Highlighting the event path to others coming around to your view is crucial- these should be actual events with dates.

**Catalysts:**

3Q22 earnings call: upward revision to management's top- line guidance for FY2022, which currently still appears to be conservative. DOL's 3Q is modeled to track around +14.5% on a 3- year stack basis (vs. street at around +10% ). If DOL can maintain the 3Q comp, then the implied 4Q comp outlook would indicate a meaningful deceleration on a 3- year stack basis (700bps+ lower than 1H22 level), based on the FY22 topline growth outlook of +6.5 - 7.5% . 4Q22 earnings call: introduction of an optimistic outlook for next FY2023: 5 - 6% comp sales vs 4 - 5% normalized comp sale range. This would be the case as (1) DOL traffic continues to improve given compelling value proposition, and (2) impact of expanded price points rolled out in 2H22, which would lead to increase in traffic in later FY22 despite mgt's current conservative view that it would be immaterial. Read- through data from WMT (focusing on Canada segment), which reports before DOL.

While we would have liked a date for WMT earnings (nit picking here), this is a very clear path (with specified events) for how we get paid as the analyst expects an upward revision to 2022 guidance next quarter along with an above trend initial 2023 SSS guidance in 4Q.

## Page 11

5) Risks

This shouldn't be an airtight thought where you put generic risks (macro, etc.), but rather a thoughtful way to be your own PM and think through how you could be wrong. Providing a mitigant to each risk shows you're truly considered key debates/risks in your recommendation.

**Risks & Mitigants**

Risk: PINS's ad outperform peer benchmarks driven by rising engagement, resulting in ARPU growth Mitigant: Pinterest's ad ROAS when adjusted for user engagement time and saturation (ad's displayed- per- user- time) still significantly trail peers. The tripology (Google, AMZN, FB) will continue optimizing their ad- suites and expand TAM focus (SMB) hurting PINS's prospects to converge to peer ARPU.

Risk: PINS' foray into e- commerce drives significant growth with Bill Ready at the helm

Mitigant: Engagement remains low vis- a- vis Instagram resulting in a disadvantageous position. E- commerce integration destroys the network moat that Pinterest would need to sustain long- term ARPU growth.

Risk: Strong MAU and ARPU growth, especially exUS

Mitigant: Growth in ROW is in primarily low GDP per capita countries and mMAUs remain low proportionally in Asia and ROW. US TAM is nearly saturated and Europe mMAU runway is coming to an end. This issue appears to be structural with FB and Twitter seeing similar trends.

This risk analysis thought through various ways the analyst's thesis could be wrong and addressed why they don't see the risks as likely outcomes.

## Page 13

1. Give away the ending up front

Long or short, price target and return, risk/reward skew, "time target", and summary of thesis

2. Thesis is non-consensus, highly differentiated or multi-faceted

Consider intellectual honesty and be wary of confirmation bias

3. The degree to which you are variant on business model drivers is clear

What is the Street missing, and to what degree are investors incorrect?

4. Primary research is insightful and supports your fundamental thesis

Describe the diligence that you've performed to build conviction in your view

5. Catalysts are discrete dates that will confirm or refute your thesis

When will investors be forced to come around to your view?

6. Risk / Reward discussion appreciates a multiple range of outcomes

Valuation helps inform the return (or loss) potential from the idea

**Common Mistakes**

**Analysts typically fall into these traps when articulating their ideas**

**Presenting too much detail**

**Presenting in chronological order**

**Incorrect:**

- Incorrect:
- Wasting time with a business description that is too detailed
- Discussing segments that do not matter to fundamentals or to share price

**Correct:**

Correct:
- Allow the questions to take you into the details
- Consider your pitch as the starting point of a more detailed conversation

**Incorrect:**

- Incorrect:
- Walking through each step of the analysis and including irrelevant details
- Finishing with the conclusion

**Correct:**

Correct:
- Starting with conclusion (thesis statement)
- Following with variant view and supporting detail from the model
- Communicating event path

## Page 15

- Your write up should be a concise outline of your thesis, variant view, event path, risks and valuation framework

- This should not be a lengthy report. You need to identify the 2-3 points that are key to the thesis and spend most of the time finding supporting evidence or data to outline why you believe what you believe.

- Properly format your charts & graphs. Axis labels, units, legend etc.

- Cite your sources. This is extremely helpful for both the reader and yourself as you revisit old pitches and update various analyses

- Your view should translate into the numbers in your model. If you think there are pricing or competitive issues this should translate into a material gross margin delta. You should be able to defend / benchmark why gross margins are going to a particular level over time. In general, if you have a long or short view on the stock it should translate into material downside or upside to a key KPI in the model.

- You will likely need to find/incorporate data from various sources to support your view

- Do not summarize sell side research. You can read sell side research after you determine your view and think through what you might have missed or need to consider. You can leverage the factual information, but you need to come up with your own view.
